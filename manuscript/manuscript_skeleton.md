# Phage-UV ecology and strain-level adaptation in anaerobic membrane biofilms

## Working title

Repeated phage-UV treatment reveals linked viral, DNA-damage response, and strain-level adaptation signatures in anaerobic membrane biofilms

## Core claim

Repeated phage-UV treatment does not need to be interpreted only as an engineering membrane-cleaning intervention. The same experiment can be used to ask how biofilm-associated microbial populations respond ecologically: through phage-host interactions, UV/DNA-damage repair programs, and strain-level SNV divergence across treatment cycles.

## Venue-neutral structure

### Abstract

- Background: anaerobic membrane biofouling, biological cleaning, and phage-UV treatment.
- Gap: limited understanding of microbial adaptation to repeated phage-UV exposure.
- Approach: integrated metagenomic, metatranscriptomic, vOTU, CRISPR-link, UV-signature, and inStrain SNV analyses.
- Results: to be filled only after staged analyses reproduce.
- Conclusion: phage-UV treatment can be evaluated as a microbial defense ecology system.

- AnMBR membrane biofouling and biological cleaning context.

### Discussion

- Treatment as ecological pressure.
- Biofilm formation and CRISPR as complementary phage-resistance context.
- UV/DNA repair as functional adaptation context.
- SNV divergence as population-structure context.
- Limitations: low replicate structure, mixed communities, no direct mutation-causality claim.
