## Abstract

Combined mechanical backwash, bacteriophage and ultraviolet C cleaning can delay membrane fouling in anaerobic membrane bioreactors, but the community-wide changes accompanying repeated cleaning remain incompletely characterised. We integrated metagenomes, metatranscriptomes and cDNA-derived 16S amplicons from one control and one phage-UV membrane, sampled in two phases across three cleaning cycles. Community profiles were resolved against 348 metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs) and a unified viral catalogue. Amplicon turnover was lower between cycles 2 and 3 than between cycles 1 and 2 in all four membrane-by-phase trajectories, whereas microbial and viral differential-abundance results depended on model specification. Cleaning cycle organised the global transcriptome geometry. Of 361,907 tested features, 7,703 differed between membranes after adjustment for phase and cycle at a false discovery rate below 0.05. Organism-level competitive tests supported 175 of 340 eligible MAGs, comprising 100 phage-UV-higher and 75 control-higher gene sets. Across the six phase-cycle cells, 6,985 genes met the recurrence criteria, with 3,334 higher in the phage-UV membrane and 3,651 higher in the control. SOS-associated genes showed a modest collective ranking shift, without support for a broad repair programme. Coverage-qualified genomic comparisons revealed organism- and sample-fraction-specific variation among five MAGs. Together, these analyses resolve widespread, bidirectional transcriptional differences within a community changing across cleaning cycles. With one membrane per condition, these findings describe the observed longitudinal system rather than a general causal treatment effect.

## Introduction closing paragraph

We therefore integrated the time-resolved MG, MT and cDNA-derived 16S data to examine three connected aspects of this system: i) microbial and viral community composition across cleaning cycles, ii) organism-resolved transcriptional differences between membranes and iii) the recurrence of gene-level differences across matched sampling phases and cycles. The complete expressed feature universe was modelled before repair and stress functions were examined as predefined gene sets. Population-genomic variation and CRISPR-derived host-phage links provided complementary organism-level context. We aimed to resolve how community composition and expressed functions varied within the observed two-membrane system.

## Discussion paragraph 1

Repeated membrane cleaning was accompanied by cycle-structured community profiles and widespread transcriptional differences between the two membranes. Cleaning cycle organised the global expression geometry, while the phase- and cycle-adjusted contrast identified 7,703 features distributed across many organisms (Figures 1-3). This community-wide analysis extends the selected-organism transcriptional observations reported for the same experiment (Myshkevych et al., 2025). Its central finding is the coexistence of temporal organisation at community level and persistent, bidirectional expression differences at organism level.

## Discussion paragraph 2

The cDNA-derived 16S profiles place the expression results within a community that changed across the cleaning sequence. Consecutive-cycle Bray-Curtis dissimilarity was lower in the second transition in all four membrane-by-phase trajectories, a pattern retained after library-total normalisation. In contrast, MAG and vOTU differential-abundance results varied with model specification (Supplementary Figure S9). These observations describe temporal turnover without establishing either community equivalence or convergence between membranes. Because the amplicons were generated from cDNA and the metagenomic profiles were relative to mapped reference collections, neither provides an absolute measure of microbial abundance.

## Discussion paragraph 3

Organism-level expression resolved differences that were not captured by a simple separation of the communities. Competitive tests supported 175 of 340 eligible MAGs, with 100 phage-UV-higher and 75 control-higher gene sets (Figure 3). Recurrence analysis recovered 6,985 genes whose directions agreed in at least five of six phase-cycle cells, again with both directions represented (Figure 4). The recurrence criterion describes persistence within the observed samples, not an independent validation. These results identify organisms with coordinated expression differences, but they do not separate changes in organism abundance from changes in transcription per cell. Jointly quantified absolute abundance and transcript output would be required to resolve that distinction.

## Discussion paragraph 4

Repair and stress functions accounted for only part of the transcriptional structure. SOS-associated genes were collectively higher-ranked in the phage-UV membrane against the complete expressed background, but their median log2 fold-change was 0.136 and none of the other seven predefined categories was supported for the same coefficient (Figure 3). The cycle-2 redox signal was not accompanied by category-level support for the cycle-3 or omnibus coefficients (Supplementary Figure S3). Functional transcription therefore identifies candidate physiological differences without demonstrating DNA lesions or a progressive repair response. Direct measurements of lesions and repair activity would be needed to test that mechanism.

## Discussion paragraph 5

The genomic and host-link analyses add organism-level context at different levels of resolution. Among the five coverage-qualified MAGs, Propionicimonas sp023458095 showed a separation between initial and cycle-3 backflush observations, compatible with fraction-specific population structure (Figure 5). CRISPR predictions connected 80 MAGs to 85 viral contigs, but none of the linked representatives met the high-quality classified-vOTU filter used for community profiling (Supplementary Figure S8). These candidate associations do not identify active infections or connect particular phages to the measured expression differences. Likewise, the selected genomic comparisons cannot distinguish population turnover from treatment-induced mutation.

## Discussion paragraph 6

The design limits inference to the two observed membranes. Treatment and membrane identity are confounded, and repeated cycles and sampling phases do not create independent treatment replicates. Model-based P values and false discovery rates characterise the fitted within-system contrasts under their assumptions; they do not estimate a general causal effect of phage-UV cleaning. Independently replicated membranes, deeper temporal sampling and paired measurements of absolute biomass, transcription and phage activity would distinguish persistent ecological differences from treatment responses. The present analysis provides a genome-resolved account of the community and expressed functions across repeated cleaning, with specific organisms and recurrent genes for that subsequent validation.
