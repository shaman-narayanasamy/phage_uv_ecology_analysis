# Cover letter draft for author review

Status: local draft only; not approved for sending or submission.

Dear Editors,

We submit the Research Article, "Heterogeneous organism-resolved
transcriptional restructuring across repeated phage-UV cleaning of anaerobic
membrane biofilms," for consideration in *ISME Communications*.

Combined bacteriophage and UV-C cleaning offers a biologically based route to
control membrane biofouling, but its community-wide consequences over repeated
cleaning remain unclear. We analysed the complete expressed-feature universe
from an anaerobic membrane bioreactor sampled across two fractions and three
cleaning cycles. The global RNA landscape changed broadly and bidirectionally,
with coherent signals distributed among organisms. SOS-response genes were
higher-ranked in the phage-UV membrane, but their median shift was small and
seven other predefined repair and stress categories were unsupported. The
results therefore identify heterogeneous, organism-resolved restructuring
rather than a uniform DNA-damage or adaptation programme.

The study is relevant to the journal's microbial-ecology audience because it
connects engineered-community composition, longitudinal transcriptome
structure, competitive functional tests, organism-level coherence, recurrent
gene behaviour, and coverage-qualified population-genomic context. The full
tested expression universe determines the analysis before functional or
taxonomic labels are used for interpretation.

We wish to foreground an important design boundary. The experiment contains
one control membrane and one phage-UV membrane, repeatedly sampled through the
cleaning cycles. Treatment is therefore confounded with membrane identity. We
present this as an intensively sampled longitudinal analysis of two membrane
histories, not as a population-level causal estimate of treatment effect. That
boundary is stated in the title-page package, Abstract, Methods, Results, and
Discussion.

The experimental system and raw sequencing accession were first reported by
Myshkevych et al. (2025), *Chemical Engineering Journal Advances* 24:100796,
doi:10.1016/j.ceja.2025.100796. The present manuscript cites that report and
uses the same public ENA study, PRJEB79569. Its new contribution is the
complete-universe transcriptome model, competitive repair and stress gene-set
tests, organism-level coherence analysis, six-cell recurrence analysis, and
coverage-qualified descriptive population genomics. We disclose this overlap
so that the editorial assessment can distinguish the new ecological analysis
from the previously reported engineering-performance and selected-gene claims.
